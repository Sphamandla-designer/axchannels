Case Study · WasteMart

Software For Work In Motion

A self-initiated product concept: a mobile app that guides waste-collection drivers through the working day, and leaves a record the office can trust afterwards.

The Problem

The Work Happens Outside. The App Has To Keep Up

A driver’s day is a run of scheduled jobs, check-ins, bin swaps, manifests, photos, barcode scans, customer sign-offs, dump and fuel records — often with patchy signal.

A mistake in one step can surface hours later, for someone who wasn’t there. So we designed for two people: the driver creating the record, and whoever reviews it afterwards. At any moment the app has to answer four questions.

  • What am I doing now?

    The active job and its next action are always the first thing on screen.

  • What’s already done?

    Progress through the day is shown, not remembered.

  • What still needs to happen?

    Missing details and pending uploads surface before they become problems.

  • What if something goes wrong?

    Mismatches, corrections and lost signal are handled inside the flow, not by stopping it.

One Job. One Next Action

The complexity lives in the system, not in the driver’s head.

  1. 01

    The workflow drives the screen

    Each job moves from assigned to checked in, captured, manifest saved and completed. The main button changes with the state, so the driver never has to work out what comes next.

    Shipped a state-driven job list the driver can reorder mid-shift.

  2. 02

    Four kinds of proof, not one

    Manifest photos, bin photos, scanned recycling bags and a drawn customer signature are separate flows, because each proves something different.

    Shipped a manifest that can’t be saved without sign-off.

  3. 03

    Flag it, don’t block it

    If a scanned bin number doesn’t match, the driver can re-check, continue or flag it for review. Finished records lock, and every correction is logged.

    Shipped non-blocking checks and an edit history on the record.

  4. 04

    Ending the day is a review

    End Day lists completed jobs, remaining work, pending uploads and failed syncs. Closing early needs a reason, so the office sees why, not just what.

    Shipped a two-step close-out, plus a visible offline and sync status.

A Complete Workflow, Not A Set Of Screens

WasteMart is a working prototype, not a deployed product. No user research, usability results or business metrics are claimed.

What we built

  • 29 interactive screens
  • A job flow from assignment to end of day
  • Four separate evidence flows
  • A logged correction trail
  • Offline and sync states
  • A token-based design system for field use

Test next

  • Put it in front of real drivers and dispatchers
  • Find which decisions hold up in the field
  • Measure close-out completeness and correction rates

The next phase, not completed work.
